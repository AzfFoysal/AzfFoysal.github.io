Flagship research

Self-Supervised Plant Disease Detection under Domain Shifts

Reproducible code ↗

A comparative study of supervised CNNs, self-supervised Vision Transformers, and a hybrid CNN–ViT architecture under label scarcity, image corruption, and laboratory-to-field domain shift.

~87KPlantVillage images
38in-domain classes
27aligned PlantDoc classes
96.3%hybrid in-domain accuracy
78%hybrid cross-domain accuracy

Question

Can self-supervised and hybrid representations improve robustness when labels are scarce and deployment images differ from laboratory data?

Models

ResNet50, DINOv2 ViT-Base, and weighted CNN–ViT feature fusion.

Stress tests

Full labels, 25% and 10% label regimes, Gaussian blur/noise, and PlantDoc cross-domain evaluation.

Interpretability

Grad-CAM and transformer attention compare local lesion evidence with broader contextual representations.

Current direction

Declared-performance validity under distribution shift

A working research framework asks when a deployed model's previously declared performance should be considered invalid as the deployment population changes.

Problem

Static accuracy declarations can become misleading when the deployment distribution changes even if the model itself is unchanged.

Method

A pre-registered sequential risk test uses random audited labels; a label-free signal increases audit intensity but does not decide the verdict.

Evidence

The public repository includes simulations, a real-classifier study, tests, result files, literature notes, and plant-disease integration.

Relevance

The work connects ML reliability with continuous monitoring and emerging AI compliance obligations.
