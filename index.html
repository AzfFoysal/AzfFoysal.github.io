About

Research depth with an engineering mindset.

I am a computer science researcher and technical professional with graduate training in computer networks and security and research experience across machine learning, computer vision, trustworthy AI, cybersecurity, and data-driven systems. My master's research at Troy University focused on making plant-disease recognition more robust when labels are scarce and real-world field images differ from laboratory training data.

My broader work explores how AI systems can remain useful under real operational constraints: distribution shift, noisy inputs, extreme class imbalance, changing threats, latency requirements, and the need for transparent or interpretable decisions. Master's Thesis · AMLDS 2026

Self-Supervised Plant Disease Detection under Domain Shifts

Comparative evaluation of ResNet50, DINOv2 ViT-Base, and a proposed hybrid CNN-ViT fusion model under full-label training, 10%/25% label scarcity, Gaussian blur/noise, and laboratory-to-field domain shift.

Reproducible code ↗
~87KPlantVillage images
38in-domain classes
27aligned PlantDoc classes
96.3%hybrid in-domain accuracy
78%hybrid cross-domain accuracy

Question

Can self-supervised and hybrid architectures generalize better when labels are limited or deployment images differ from curated training data?

Approach

ResNet50 + DINOv2 ViT-Base + weighted CNN-ViT feature fusion, evaluated across label efficiency, corruption robustness, and cross-domain generalization.

Interpretability

Grad-CAM and transformer attention visualizations compare local lesion evidence with broader global context.
